# Review brief: small128 iter-1 regression — recipe diagnosis

**Ask:** rank the candidate causes, propose the cheapest discriminating experiments, and
recommend a concrete training recipe. The teacher and data are measured-good (below); we
believe the TRAINING RECIPE is wrong and want it fixed, not worked around. Our bar for a
healthy run: **even epoch 1 should be slightly better than the base model.**

## Setup

**Game:** Color Lines 98 (9×9, 7 colors, stochastic spawns, single-player).
Empirically score ≈ 2.03 × turns survived (score=survival; all scalar value targets
saturate — long-established in this project). "Floor" = early deaths = the target metric.

**Base model (being improved): `ep87`** — 10b×128ch policy-only ResNet, 3.0M params.
It is a DISTILLED STUDENT: trained from scratch to copy a 4×-larger teacher (pillar3k,
11.9M) with 0.5·soft-CE + 0.5·hard-CE(teacher argmax), T=0.5, batch 4096, lr 1e-3
cosine 100 epochs. Teacher-match plateaued at 73% argmax / 96% top-3 while gameplay kept
climbing (mimicry-vs-capacity decoupling). Base eval (500 held-out seeds, greedy, fp16):
**mean 12,987 / P50 8,889 / P5 1,082 / P10 1,799 / <1000 4.8%**. Prior top-share
P50 = **0.617** (it is a DECISIVE policy — the hard-CE blend did that).

**Teacher (data generator): MCTS on ep87's own priors** — 1600–2400 sims, PUCT c=2.5,
q_weight=1.0, top-30 legal-softmax expansion, batch-8 virtual loss, leaf value = a
27-feature linear board evaluator (no NN value head; the base is policy-only). Root
Dirichlet(0.3)@0.25 during generation. **Teacher strength, measured at the generation
config** (500 selfplay games, cap 1500 turns, WITH exploration noise) vs base greedy
(same cap): P1 929 vs 514, **P5 2,865 vs 1,167 (+146%, near the ~3,050 cap)**,
P10 2,971 vs 1,963, deaths-within-1500t **5.6% vs ~15-20%**. The teacher is a
~2×-floor-class teacher. NOT in question.

**Corpus: 2,974,799 states, 75/25 crisis/selfplay, all generated by the teacher above.**
- crisis (2.24M): probe base greedily to death → rewind 15 turns (replay @2400 sims,
  36% escape) and 50 turns (replay @1600, 82% escape) → record every replay move.
  Escape-or-die states; anchors span turns ~800–12,400.
- selfplay (0.73M): 500 fresh games @1600 sims, temp=1.0 first 15 moves, cap 1500 turns.
- Targets: per state, top-5 moves by MCTS visits, visit-normalized (`pol_values`, sum=1).
  Training CE is over the full 6561-softmax with the dense scatter of these 5.
- Decisiveness (top-share of the top-5 target): P50 0.441 raw; 63% of states > 0.40.
  (The pillar3k-era corpus was 15-22% > 0.40 — ours is much sharper, because sims are
  1600-2400 vs mostly 600.)

**Recipe (the exact one that worked for pillar3k on a 256ch base):**
```
train_path_b --resume ep87 --warm-start --channels 128 --amp --compile
  --epochs 28 --batch-size 32768 --lr 3e-4 --warmup-epochs 1
  --target-temperature 0.7 --decisiveness-power 3.0
```
- `--target-temperature 0.7`: targets **(1/0.7) (sharpening).
- `--decisiveness-power 3.0` (dw3): per-state CE weight = (target top-share)³,
  mean-1 normalized over the batch population.
- Precedent: this recipe took pillar3f (256ch, top-share 0.40) +18% mean / +28% floor
  on a 70/30 crisis corpus (HISTORY 173-174). Best epochs 17-22 of 28.

## Probes (all on 5,000 sampled corpus states, same sample throughout)

1. **No de-peaking.** Top-share on the target support: ep87 0.617 → ep7 0.621 → ep14
   0.623. Unchanged, incl. on the decisive subset. (The dw3+T0.7 defense held.)
2. **Adoption STUCK.** Teacher argmax ≠ base argmax on 12.9% of states ("corrections").
   Of those, the trained model adopts the teacher's move **26.1% at ep7 → 25.0% at
   ep14**. Overall argmax match to base: 88.3% → 88.4%. Val plateaued (1.978 → 1.973).
   Training has CONVERGED to an equilibrium by ~ep7.
3. **Collateral drift.** On AGREEMENT states (teacher confirms base), the trained model
   picks a *third* move **8.4%** of the time (stable ep7→ep14). Near-tie argmax flips
   from redistribution pressure, presumably mirrored off-corpus → the floor damage.
4. **THE GRADIENT-BUDGET FINDING (we believe this is the smoking gun — please verify
   the reasoning).** dw3 weights on this corpus: P50 0.64, P90 2.21, max 7.1 (weak
   dynamic range — the corpus is uniformly sharp). Split by agreement:
   - agreement states (87.1%): **95.4% of total gradient weight**
   - disagreement states (12.9%): **4.6%** (0.36× mean — dw3 DOWN-weights them)
   - decisive disagreements (2.7%): 2.0%
   Mechanism: on correction states the visits are SPLIT between the teacher's pick and
   the base's prior-backed pick → lower top-share → dw3 treats "contested" as
   "indecisive" and starves exactly the states that carry the new information. On the
   pillar3k corpus (flat quiet bulk + sharp escapes, weaker prior) decisiveness
   correlated WITH signal; here it anti-correlates. Meanwhile 95% of gradient re-fits
   agreement states toward T0.7-sharpened targets whose top-share (P50 0.544) is still
   FLATTER than the base (0.617) → confidence redistribution → probe-3 drift.

## Prior art in this project (so you don't re-suggest known-dead ends)

- On the 256ch lineage: uniform soft-CE (T=1.0/0.5/0.25), completed-Q/Gumbel targets,
  hard decision-BC on overrides, KL-anchor trust region — ALL regressed a strong base
  on 400-sim data. dw3 + T0.7 on a 70/30 crisis corpus is the only full-corpus recipe
  that ever beat a strong base (+18%). Deep-search (4800) targeted corrections via
  task-arithmetic also worked (+36%) but is a different, surgical form.
- Value heads: policy-only by design; every scalar value target saturates
  (score=survival). A pairwise-ranking value head trained on this base's 128ch backbone
  hit val_acc ≈ 0.52 (random) — the 256ch backbone gets 0.64 on identical data.
- The base itself absorbed a 3.85M-state distillation corpus fine (its own training),
  so "3M states is too much data for 3M params" is not obviously true.

## Hypotheses for ranking (H1 = ours; please attack it)

- **H1 — dw3 selectivity inversion (numbers above).** Fix directions: weight by
  DISAGREEMENT (w = 1 + γ·1[argmax(target) ≠ argmax(base)]), or dw computed only on
  contested states, or drop decisive agreements from the corpus (they carry ~no new
  information), or dw on (top-share × disagreement-margin).
- **H2 — T=0.7 sharpening is wrong here:** targets are already sharp (P50 0.44 raw);
  sharpened agreements (0.544) are still flatter than the base (0.617) so the bulk
  gradient exerts flattening/redistribution pressure. Candidate: T=1.0 (no sharpening)
  or per-state T. Note the pillar3k geometry differed (base 0.40 vs sharpened ~0.30 and
  it still worked — why?).
- **H3 — no hard anchor:** the base's decisiveness came from a 0.5 hard-CE blend; iter1
  is pure soft-CE. Candidate: blend hard-CE toward the TARGET argmax, or toward the
  BASE argmax on agreements (self-anchor).
- **H4 — lr/batch shock:** 3e-4 @ 32768 on a student whose whole history is batch 4096.
  Counter-evidence: adoption STUCK (not thrashing), peakedness stable. But the 8.4%
  drift could be lr-scale. Candidate: 1e-4 or 3e-5.
- **H5 — anchor distribution gap:** the 25% selfplay share is capped at 1500 turns
  (early-game boards only); pillar3k's selfplay anchor was uncapped (mean ~9k turns).
  If board distribution is not fully stationary in turn, mid/late quiet play is
  unanchored while 75% crisis pulls style toward danger boards.
- **H6 — Dirichlet in the recorded targets:** every generation move had root
  Dirichlet(0.3)@0.25; its noise is baked into the visit targets (same as the pillar3k
  corpus, so probably not decisive — but sims differ).
- **H7 — interference/capacity:** the 3M student cannot absorb 13% exceptions without
  breaking neighbors (adoption plateau ≈ the old 73% match plateau). We de-prioritize
  this (the base absorbed bigger corpora; the gradient-budget finding suffices to
  explain the data) but list it for completeness.
